> It's been years, possibly even a full decade, since a new technological fad was meaningfully *good*: since it offered something to make our lives unambiguously better, even if it came with a cost.

<!--sep-->...

> We're forced to confront the fact that the tech leaders that have been lauded by society for so long are just plain *evil*: that they've connived in genocides, want to ethnically cleanse the very countries we're living in, and that they're personally terrible people.

<!--sep-->...

> In such circumstances, at the very least, we have to acknowledge that fracture, obscenity and the shattering of the way we see the world is what we're facing: it does no good for us to pretend that it is anything other than something that we simply can't put into words or symbols.

<!--sep-->...

> Developing a keen sense of patience is likewise an important discipline. A revelation may well come, and we can watch in hope for one. But it may tarry, and revelations tend to arise in their own time: we may be waiting for a very long while. Being able to wait without frustration and keep doing things while we're waiting is important to stay sane: if the day of judgement comes, after all, we should wish to be found doing our duty.
