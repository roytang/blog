Our friend from the US was back again for a few weeks recently, which means our normally remote gaming group got together a few times in person to play some boardgames, these are the ones we played.

### The Lord of the Rings Trick-Taking Game (Fellowship and Two Towers)

The full title for these is super clunky. BGG: [The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring – Trick-Taking Game](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/429293/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-fellowship-of-the-ring-t), [The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers – Trick-Taking Game](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/448271/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-two-towers-trick-taking)

This was interesting, it's' basically a trick-taking game like bridge, except all 4 players are cooperating to accomplish each character's objectives. Each game has 18 chapters and each chapter has a different set of characters to play and each character has different objectives like winning a certain number of tricks or winning some number of a certain suit or getting the first trick or the final trick and so on.

These two games took most of our time. It was pretty fun, though we failed a lot and had to redo many of the chapters. The worst one was chapter 34 which we failed FIVE TIMES before realizing we were counting the tricks wrong and after which we failed FIVE MORE TIMES before finally getting there! That one chapter took us like three hours lol.

Each chapter is themed around specific happenings in the first two LOTR books and had some flavor read out at the start. There were a lot of obscure characters that weren't even in the film. One of our friends had read the books too many times and was complaining that most of the lines being read weren't even in the books!

The final chapter based on Return of the King is planned to be released early next year so we are looking forward to playing through that when our friend comes back next year!

### Guards of Atlantis II

BGG: [Guards of Atlantis II](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/267609/guards-of-atlantis-ii)

I mistakenly called this "Guards of Atlanta" before we played it and we ended up calling it that most of the time. (Though at one point we did jokingly say stuff like "Heroes of Arizona" which sounds like a complately different game).

This was a team-based game with teams of two competing with 2-3 players each. It's basically modelled after DOTA and other MOBA-type games. Each team starts with a number of minions in the middle of the map and each player plays a hero that helps your team push forward or defend as needed. Once one side's minions are wiped out, the team pushes forward, and the game ends if one team manages to push all the way to the enemy base or has killed enemy heroes enough times.

Each hero starts with 5 ability cards, 3 of which are upgradeable. Each hero gains experience and levels up and gains upgraded versions of his ability cards. 

We tried this right after we finished the two LOTR games but didn't have time to finish our first game, where I played Wasp the Warmaiden. We came back into it after a few days and this time I chose Rowenna the Vanguard from the Arcane expansion. Our team lost the first game but we won the second one, so we finished even.

### Wyrmspan

BGG: [Wyrmspan](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/410201/wyrmspan)

This is a spinoff/variant of *Wingspan*, which we played a bit [back in 2022](/2022/11/boardgame-day/#wingspan) and also played [the Steam version](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1054490/Wingspan/) online a few times. Instead of collecting birds, in this game you're an adventurer collecting dragons!

The rules are a bit different but the game largely plays the same way. Though I think I like the flavor of this one more. Similar to Wingspan though I went through our one playthrough not really knowing WTH I was doing and I just kept buying the biggest dragons I could. The others were doing so much stuff like collecting resources and tucking so many cards I thought for sure I was doing so poorly, but when we added up the points at the end of the game I ended up winning the thing!

I forgot to take pictures of the actual game, but they gave me the scoresheet afterwards. One hundred percent win rate!

### Inis 

BGG: [Inis](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/155821/inis)

This is a celtic-themed game where you are all competing to becoming king (or something like that) by placing troops, exploring new territories to expand the map, playing sanctuaries and such. Each round the players drafts 4 action cards each from a deck of 17 cards (one is set aside unused) and get bonus cards for controlled territories or for earning epic tales. It's the sort of game where the first game takes quite a bit longer while everyone learns the rules and what the cards are, but actual play goes by quickly once you have everthing down. Our first game took about an hour, but the second one took only around 30 minutes I think. Unfortunately I won neither game, though I was a pretender both times. 

It was pretty fun and feels like the sort of game where you need to play it quite a few games to really figure out the strategies. Fantastic art too.

### Modern Art (PH edition)

BGG: [Modern Art (1992 original)](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/118/modern-art)

[PH edition on GamingLib](https://www.gaminglib.com/products/modern-art-ph)

I found out only while writing this post that the original version of this game came out in 1992. Our friend bought the PH edition which features five PH artists, of whom I was only familiar wth Larry Alcala.

This is a competitive bidding game where players start with P100M and bid on artworks to drive their value up and earn more money, with the goal of ending the four rounds with the most money. In the first game we had no idea what we were doing and one of us managed to buy a bunch of late-round artworks on discount to run away with the game. We played a second game where I had a better idea of what was going on and managed to control the values of the artworks enough to eventually win the game! Each game took less than an hour.
