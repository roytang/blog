A friend was helping his aunt sell a used iPad, barely used and a generation newer than my current one. My current iPad's battery has been degraded already, dropping quite a lot after a single session, so I was interested in the upgrade, especially since the price was pretty cheap. My friend tested the iPad and confirmed the battery was good which was my main concern so I was good to go with the transaction. But there's things about buying a used iPad (and any iDevice I suppose) that I wish I had known about it ahead of time, hence this post.

The trouble started after the meetup where I got the iPad. My friend said they had done a reset and it turned on so I took it home and started doing the set up. But during the set up process, I ran into the problem: the iPad had [activation lock](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108794) enabled and I needed to input an iCloud account and password. My friend's aunt helpfully provided her creds but apparently they were NOT the same as the account needed to unlock the iPad. We tried a few workarounds over a few days including logging into her iCloud and removing the device from the account (I'm not sure how it even got there, given the rest of this post), emailing Apple support to ask them to deactivate the lock, but none proved helpful.

Some [reddit threads](https://old.reddit.com/r/ipad/comments/zs0n7o/can_i_do_anything_with_a_bunch_of_old_ipads_90_of/j16dj1e/) told us there was an option to bypass the activation lock using some third-party tools that honestly seemed super shady. I hesitated to try them myself. The friend's aunt told us we could have the bypass done at Greenhills (the local center of used device sales and sometimes shady deals) and it turns out that's where she bought it and she had a reset done before. She agreed to cover the cost of any extra work I have done.

When I had time I went there to a shop a friend trusted and spoke to the guy and he said the bypass will take around 40 minutes and that afterwards I wouldn't be able to reset the device again, otherwise the activation lock would be a problem again. I approved the procedure and a bit less than an hour later I had a working and usable iPad.

When I got home I did some setup, installing my usual apps and so on. Then I ran into problem #2: I was unable to login to my iCloud account. After flailing around a bit I found some Youtube videos suggesting this was a known effect of the kind of bypass that was done. There were some recommended workarounds once again with some shady software. (I found a reddit thread just flat out claiming the suggested software was malware.) It did not seem like a good idea to try it.

I was still able to log in to the App Store so I had access to my previous purchases so that part was fine. I eventually decided that not having access to iCloud while not ideal was acceptable given how cheaply I got the device and ultimately I could live with it, so we closed the transaction there.

So I understand the activation lock is an anti-theft measure, so that stolen devices are not useful to the thieves. And I wish it weren't so, but I suspect this is a typical use case for the sort of used devices sold at places like Greenhills, which is why the techs there are so used to doing this sort of bypass. I guess at least it provides a way for people to have access to cheaper devices, but it's not an ideal economy when it incentivizes this sort of thing.

It's not my first dance with this kind of thing: a few years ago our helper had an Android phone that needed a factory reset but it required a Google account as well, and I was never able to solve that so it ended up just being a brick that's still in this room somewhere. I don't think I looked into any shady/hacky means of bypassing it back then.

This is the first time I tried purchasing a used device myself and while I am able to use the device for my usual reading purposes (I can confirm the battery is great!), I do wish I had known ahead of time about the limitations that come with buying a "used" device, even if the price was really good. 

I will probably think twice the next time, but here's a TLDR for future me (and anyone else who might be reading this): when buying a used device, make sure it's not locked!

Also: If I ever sell a used device (hasn't happened yet), I should make sure to properly reset it and remove it from my account so that future owners won't have a problem!
