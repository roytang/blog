#### Breath of Fire II

I would guess that I worked on this one after playing through and enjoying Breath of Fire III on the PSX. This one is a much more primitive game by comparison, mostly just a classic turn-based JRPG wrapped around the usual "mysterious boy is actually a dragon" story. Not much to say about it.

#### Lufia II: Rise of the Sinistrals

Probably the most "hidden gem" among the titles here, the series is no longer very well-known in the present day. The series has not had a new release since the NDS remake of this game.

The game is a rather traditional JRPG with turn-based combat similar to the Dragon Quest games, with a few distinct features that make it stand out in my memory:

- Inside dungeons, enemies are visible (instead of the traditional random encounters of the time) and movement is turn-based, i.e. whenever your party moves, the enemies will move also, and contact will start a combat.
- A strong focus on puzzles in the dungeons. The culmination of this is a puzzle the game calls "the world's most difficult trick", which is a sliding blocks puzzle akin to [Klotski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klotski).
- The game also includes an optional 99-floor procedurally generated dungeon called the Ancient Cave. Attempting this dungeon is something like playing a roguelike: you are stripped of all levels and items and must make your way to the bottom floor to claim the dungeon's prize. Dying meant getting kicked out of the run but you can try again. You can also acquire items that can be brought into the dungeon for future runs. At the time this seemed amazing and the Ancient Cave felt like a whole game-within-a-game!

It's too bad I don't have saves of this game, but I'm pretty sure I cleared the ancient cave too!

I never played the NDS remake of this game, but apparently that one was an action RPG which I imagine would not have felt the same!

#### Tales of Phanstasia

Finished on [2002/04/13](/2002/04/someday-well-all-look-back-and-laugh/)

Playtime: Around 30 hours.

Favorite character: Arche.

This [blog post from 2002](https/2002/04/someday-well-all-look-back-and-laugh/) says I finished Tales of Phantasia after around half a year of playing it, which is a long time for a total playtime of 30 hours!

This is the second Tales games I've played, presumably starting it after I played **Tales of Destiny** on PS1 and really enjoyed that game. Phantasia is older and the first game of the series so it's kind of rougher than Destiny, but I still enjoyed it a lot, enough to finish the game though I didn't get through all the sidequests. The story involves a Mana Tree, which feels very derivative of games like Secret of Mana, but also includes some sci-fi elements like time travel and another world, which is not unusual for a Tales game.

I was able to load my save and just picked up my team and headed into the final boss and beat him quite easily even when I didn't really know what I was doing! That's probably an indication that I was very overlevelled lol. Cless was at level 90 and 9999 HP! Honestly Arche just spamming Meteor Storm and Indignation was enough to win the game. 

This game did not have an English release, so I played it via [a popular fan translation patch by DeJap](https://dejap.eludevisibility.org/top.php). (Their website was last updated in 2003 but is still up! Amazing.) The translation of the ending seemed decent enough!

#### Dragon Quest V

Last played/Finished?: 2001/12/09 (based on the timestamp of the save state)

I already wrote about this game a bit when [I wrote about the NDS version last year](/2025/05/nds-games/#dragon-quest-v-hand-of-the-heavenly-bride). One thing to note is that the NDS version looks much much better than this SNES version which looks very barebones and dated!

This game was not available in English on the SNES/SFamicom (the NDS version was the first English release). I instead played this one via a fan translation from dejap - I was able to find the appropriate ROM [here](https://www.emu-land.net/en/consoles/snes/roms/dragon-quest-v-tenkuu-no-hanayome) and used it to load the SRAM. The final save does have access to the final dungeon already, so presumably I did finish the game then.

Since I had already written about the NDS version, I made some comparisons between both saves:

- In both games, I named the protagonist "Guy".
- In both saves, the protagonist is at level 37! However, I think the party members in the NDS version are more evenly leveled.
- In the SNES version, the protagonist had a choice of marrying either Bianca or Fiona. In my save I chose Bianca.
- In the NDS version, a third potential bride is introduced named Debora, and she is the one chosen in my save. (Poor Fiona never got chosen!)
- On SNES, I named the male child "Bob". On NDS I named him "Parry".
- On SNES, I named the female child "Cay". On NDS I named her "Madchen" (no idea why).
- A lot of the names of towns and characters are different between the Dejap translation and the official English NDS release. The Dejap version sticks more closely to romanized versions of the Japanese names. For example a town is named "Oracleberry" in the Dejap version while it's "Fortuna" in the English version.

I think this is so far the only Dragon Quest I enjoyed enough to play through twice?!?

Last played: 2000/09/22
