This is my set recap for the **Marvel Super Heroes** MTG set! It has only been around six weeks since [I last wrote one of these](/2026/07/mtg-sos/)! This pace of set releases continues to be exhausting.

<!--more-->

### SOS Limited Summary / Notes

- MSH was *not* a well-balanced limited format. Blue and white were much stronger than the other colors, with Azorius being the best archetype by far. I ended up playing a third of my events with UW.
- Despite that, my best win rate was with RW even though red was kind of a weaker color here. That may have just been a small sample size, as I only drafted that archetype twice.
- I played slightly less events than usual, but not by much. My win rate was slightly above my average.
- The set feels less synergy-focused than SOS, making play fundamentals more important.
- Lots of bomby uncommons, so you're always facing a lot of cards that are must-kills.
- Tri-color was a bit viable, but better if you had green, as it had a lot of "any color" mana sources like {{< card >}}Undercover Skrull{{< /card >}}, {{< card >}}Doc Samson, Super Psychiatrist{{< /card >}} in addition to basic land cyclers and lands like {{< card >}}Surveillance Room{{< /card >}}

### Mechanics

- Power-Up: A variant of exhaust plus kicker that lets creatures be viable both early and late. I screwed up a couple of times with this early on because I thought the first-turn cost was discounted by the amount you paid, but actually the discount was for the card's mana cost. This mostly matters for cards like {{< card >}}She-Hulk, Jade Defender{{< /card >}} where the power-up cost has more color pips than the mana cost. So a couple of times the autotapper used my only two green lands to cast the card and was left wondering why I couldn't play the power-up cost afterwards.
- Teamwork: An extra cost on instants and sorceries to tap creatures with a certain total power. It's kind of like crew for instants and sorceries! The implementation was fairly good, and it plays well with vigilance. It's not a build-around, with only {{< card >}}Agent Maria Hill{{< /card >}} explicitly naming the mechanic. Supposedly this is the UW draft archetype but it's not strictly necessary for UW decks to work.
- Connive: A returning mechanic used on villains in the set, which is a thematic win.

### Flavor

- I am a big Marvel fan of course, and I was happy with a lot of the implementations and deep cuts here. The fact that B-list super heroes like {{< card >}}Justice, Vance Astrovik{{< /card >}} and Hazmat ({{< card >}}Hero in Training{{< /card >}}) were limited format all-stars is great.
- I had one game where Galactus was coming and I couldn't do anything about it. I lost but it felt like an amazing flavor win.
- Favorite card design: {{< card >}}Speedball{{< /card >}}. A unique and innovative protection ability that actually fits in red and conveys Speedball's powers flavorfully.

A fun thing they did for this set was adding a limited-time set of custom achievements. You could mostly get them by playing the set's limited format, except for that one achievement that required you to play spiders; I made a {{< card >}}Spider-Girl, Legacy Hero{{< /card >}} brawl deck to get there easily.
