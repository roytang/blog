---
title: "Who Wants to Live Forever?"
date: 2026-10-13T17:29:46+08:00
---

I read someone's post yesterday about how they don't understand people who want to live forever. That they are glad that their life will eventually come to an end.

I can certainly appreciate that sentiment: living is hard and can feel like a burden and the world sucks, especially in our current timeline. And I even feel low enough sometimes that I just want to retreat from everything. And I know that someday, all things must end. Eventually.

But I still find myself occasionally fantasizing about living forever. Not an absolute immortality obviously, more of "immune to age and disease" and not "still alive floating through space during the heat death of the universe" immortality. I'm not insane.

My main motivation about wanting to live forever is my continuing annoyance at not having enough time to do all the things I want to do, read the books I want to read, play the games I want to play, watch all the movies and TV I want to watch, and so on. I always tell myself if I ever become a supervillain, my origin story would involve trying to become immortal.

Outliving everyone you know and watching them all grow old and die is going to suck obviously (the Superman problem), but that's the counterpart of worrying about what happens to your loved ones after your die, so that's kind of a wash I guess? 

And I know all of this is just a fantasy and not going to happen, but the most important thing about being immortal would be seeing where all of this goes. Has humanity screwed up too much or can we still recover? Are we headed for a Star Trek future or a Terminator one? I want to know!

---

One aspect of immortality that I feel is often not considered in fiction is that I think it's very likely that immortality would slowly drive one insane. For the same reason that I think billionaires are also at least slightly insane: a life divorced from the concerns of ordinary people gradually warps your perspective so that you are unable to understand the thinking of normal people. And that's just billionaires with a normal lifetime, I can only imagine that living hundreds of years means your thinking is vastly different from most people. This may or may not mean insanity, but consider also that human brains would probably not have evolved to consider centuries worth of experiences!
