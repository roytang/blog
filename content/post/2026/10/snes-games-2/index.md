### Intro

Last time I wrote about SNES games that I managed to play/finish with an actual cartridge. Today I'm writing about SNES games I played purely through an emulator. Back then my emulator of choice was [ZSNES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZSNES), which seems to have stopped development back in 2007. I'm still able to load the old SRAM files via Snes9x, but I am having trouble loading the individual ZSNES save state files.

#### Final Fantasy IV

(Also known as Final Fantasy II for the original US SNES release.)

FF4 felt like Squaresoft finally starting to expand its storytelling in their flagship RPG series to include character drama, and boy do we get a lot of character drama. There's romance, betrayal, honor and duty, a disapproving father-in-law, a spoony bard, a fall from grace, a redemption, more than one sacrifice, coming back from the dead, and so on.

I don't find the gameplay much to write home about. It's still the classic ATB style of course, but each character has a fixed "job" and set of abilities that they learn as they level up. The random encounter rate could be quite tedious. This was definitely a story-focused entry rather than a gameplay-focused one.

While I'm certain I finished this via the emulator (it's on the 2002 linked above), I suspect I only played through it once and I don't think I ever played any of the later versions like the NDS 3D remake. I did at least try the 2001 *Final Fantasy Chronicles* version on PSX, but it seems unlikely I finished it there. 

#### Final Fantasy V

I much enjoyed FF5 (which never had a US release on the SNES) over FF4, which means my likely ranking for the SNES trilogy is FF6 > FF5 > FF4, which feels like a good progression.

The story and characters are not as elaborate as FF4; it's basically a "save the crystals" thing. But this one was a gameplay-focused iteration, and it had a pretty sweet job system. Each crystal your team finish unlocks new jobs and each of the 4 MCs can take any job they like. I just loved mastering those jobs, and I believe I even tried to do complete job mastery for every character (not sure if I succeeded). 

I played enough of the game to beat both of the optional superbosses in the game, Omega and Shinryu. And I found out just now that the Game Boy Advance version (which I never played) also added a bonus postgame dungeon with even stronger superbosses, wow!

And while I did finish it on a emulator, I believe I also played the *Final Fantasy Anthology* version and probably finished that version as well. I'm sure I got fairly far with it on the PSX because my younger brothers were of the age where I could get them to do a few minutes of grinding for me before I let them play whatever, and they'd just be happy to be holding the controller lol. 

#### Breath of Fire II

I would guess that I worked on this one after playing through and enjoying Breath of Fire III on the PSX. This one is a much more primitive game by comparison, mostly just a classic turn-based JRPG wrapped around the usual "mysterious boy is actually a dragon" story. Not much to say about it.

#### Lufia II: Rise of the Sinistrals

Probably the most "hidden gem" among the titles here, the series is no longer very well-known in the present day. The series has not had a new release since the NDS remake of this game.

The game is a rather traditional JRPG with turn-based combat similar to the Dragon Quest games, with a few distinct features that make it stand out in my memory:

- Inside dungeons, enemies are visible (instead of the traditional random encounters of the time) and movement is turn-based, i.e. whenever your party moves, the enemies will move also, and contact will start a combat.
- A strong focus on puzzles in the dungeons. The culmination of this is a puzzle the game calls "the world's most difficult trick", which is a sliding blocks puzzle akin to [Klotski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klotski).
- The game also includes an optional 99-floor procedurally generated dungeon called the Ancient Cave. Attempting this dungeon is something like playing a roguelike: you are stripped of all levels and items and must make your way to the bottom floor to claim the dungeon's prize. Dying meant getting kicked out of the run but you can try again. You can also acquire items that can be brought into the dungeon for future runs. At the time this seemed amazing and the Ancient Cave felt like a whole game-within-a-game!

It's too bad I don't have saves of this game, but I'm pretty sure I cleared the ancient cave too!

I never played the NDS remake of this game, but apparently that one was an action RPG which I imagine would not have felt the same!

I've taken so long to write this post (it's been in my drafts for more than 2 months), that in the interim, [a remake of Lufia 1 and 2 has been announced for modern platforms](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4027620/Lufia_I__II_The_Sinistrals_Saga/)! The first Lufia game was kind of mid (and I probably never got too far with it), so I have mixed feeling about newer generations who might try this new version out and give up after the first game and not experience Lufia 2! IDK if I'd replay it again myself. It looks to be a straight port and does not look like they are adding any QoL improvements, I suppose we'll see.

#### Tales of Phanstasia

Finished on [2002/04/13](/2002/04/someday-well-all-look-back-and-laugh/)

Playtime: Around 30 hours.

Favorite character: Arche.

This [blog post from 2002](/2002/04/someday-well-all-look-back-and-laugh/) says I finished Tales of Phantasia after around half a year of playing it, which is a long time for a total playtime of 30 hours!

This is the second Tales games I've played, presumably starting it after I played **Tales of Destiny** on PS1 and really enjoyed that game. Phantasia is older and the first game of the series so it's kind of rougher than Destiny, but I still enjoyed it a lot, enough to finish the game though I didn't get through all the sidequests. The story involves a Mana Tree, which feels very derivative of games like Secret of Mana, but also includes some sci-fi elements like time travel and another world, which is not unusual for a Tales game.

I was able to load my save and just picked up my team and headed into the final boss and beat him quite easily even when I didn't really know what I was doing! That's probably an indication that I was very overlevelled lol. Cless was at level 90 and 9999 HP! Honestly Arche just spamming Meteor Storm and Indignation was enough to win the game. 

This game did not have an English release, so I played it via [a popular fan translation patch by DeJap](https://dejap.eludevisibility.org/top.php). (Their website was last updated in 2003 but is still up! Amazing.) The translation of the ending seemed decent enough!

#### Dragon Quest V

Last played/Finished?: 2001/12/09 (based on the timestamp of the save state)

I already wrote about this game a bit when [I wrote about the NDS version last year](/2025/05/nds-games/#dragon-quest-v-hand-of-the-heavenly-bride). One thing to note is that the NDS version looks much much better than this SNES version which looks very barebones and dated!

This game was not available in English on the SNES/SFamicom (the NDS version was the first English release). I instead played this one via a fan translation from dejap - I was able to find the appropriate ROM [here](https://www.emu-land.net/en/consoles/snes/roms/dragon-quest-v-tenkuu-no-hanayome) and used it to load the SRAM. The final save does have access to the final dungeon already, so presumably I did finish the game back then.

Since I had already written about the NDS version, I made some comparisons between both saves:

- In both games, I named the protagonist "Guy".
- In both saves, the protagonist is at level 37! However, I think the party members in the NDS version are more evenly leveled.
- In the SNES version, the protagonist had a choice of marrying either Bianca or Fiona. In my save I chose Bianca.
- In the NDS version, a third potential bride is introduced named Debora, and she is the one chosen in my save. (Poor Fiona never got chosen! I suppose if I ever do a replay I should give her a chance.)
- On SNES, I named the male child "Bob". On NDS I named him "Parry".
- On SNES, I named the female child "Cay". On NDS I named her "Madchen" (no idea why).
- A lot of the names of towns and characters are different between the Dejap translation and the official English NDS release. The Dejap version sticks more closely to romanized versions of the Japanese names. For example a town is named "Oracleberry" in the Dejap version while it's "Fortuna" in the English version.

I think this is so far the only Dragon Quest I enjoyed enough to play through twice?!?

#### Seiken Densetsu III

Last played: 2000/09/22

Playtime: < 18 hours (unfinished).

This section was originally supposed to be SNES games I **finished** on an emulator, and I put Seiken Densetsu III here because it was listed on the saves page as "Near the end". But after loading my save in an emulator it does not feel like I am anywhere close to the end of the game. The playtime is at less than 18 hours, and HLTB lists main story at 24hrs and my characters haven't even undergone the second class change yet, so I guess I have NOT actually finished this game.

After loading the save I did try to run around and see how far I am from actually finishing the game, but I just have no idea and didn't have the patience for it, so I guess the SNES version will remain unfinished. I played this game using a fan translation patch, since it never came out in NA, but it has already had a remake named **Trials of Mana** which I think had decent reviews, so maybe I'll get around to that someday.
