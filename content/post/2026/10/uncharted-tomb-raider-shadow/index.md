Like many young male gamers of a certain age, back in the day I did try playing the original Tomb Raider games a bit but never got particularly far or even enjoyed them really. This was back at the dawn of 3d gaming, and it would be another generation or two before camera controls would become good enough for me to log significant playtime in action games of this nature. In fact, I have never seen any of the Tomb Raider films either, so my knowledge of this franchise and its lore is limited to what occasionally leaks from the internet.

As such, my first serious foray into the whole archeological action-adventure subgenre actual began with Nathan Drake and the *Uncharted* games. I was originally planning to write about the Tomb Raider games only, but the Uncharted games are kind of the same and thematically related and I haven't written about the first two Uncharted games before so I decided to write some words about them here as well.

### Uncharted 2: Among Thieves (2009)

This was the first game of this kind I played, and I finished a run over the course of a mere 7 days! (Trophy dates are great for getting this kind of data.) And looking back at this from 2026, there is a bit of a mystery: how did I play this game? I don't own a physical copy, I don't have a record of a digital purchase, and it was not listed as a free PS+ game before 2020. The likely answer is that we borrowed it, though I don't have any records of convos from that time to confirm that either. Ah well.

The Uncharted games follow the adventures of Nathan Drake, kind of a roguish Indiana Jones type "archaeologist" hunting for treasure. Each game covers his quest to find a particular mythical treasure/location. For this game, he is searching for the mythical kingdom of Shambhala. Usually the antagonists are some kind of well-armed rival party also seeking whatever you are seeking.

In terms of gameplay, these are third-person action games that involve exploration/traversal (where you try to make your way across a level, usually involving a lot of climbing and jumping and other things that require someone to be ridiculously physically fit), combat/gunplay (where Nathan Drake kills many many people, it's so ridiculous just how many people he ends up killing), some stealth sequences, some light puzzle solving, and lots of big exciting action setpieces and chase sequences and similar things. The pacing of these games is always very good, alternating between different types of gameplay so you don't get too bored one way or another.

The Uncharted games are structured in linear chapters where you mostly can't go back to previous chapters and areas after you've done them, so things like completing all collectibles would be a challenge.

My favorite thing about this game is that it had an unforgettable opening sequence: The game starts in media res with Nathan Drake hanging precariously from a derailed train that is in itself hanging precariously off a miles-high cliff. You have to guide Drake up through the remnants of the train to safety. I would find out later that these near-death escapes are stock and trade for Nathan Drake and later Lara Croft, but Uncharted 2's opening sequence will forever be seared into my memory because it was the first of its kind for me.

In fact, I daresay that I still consider Uncharted 2 to be the best of the 4 games I will discuss in this post. Fantastic graphics too.

### Tomb Raider (2013)

> Finished Tomb Raider. Not gonna go for achievement completion since there's multiplayer stuff. 100% collectibles is good enough!

{{< photos 956354182767820805 >}}

Looks like I got this one off a Steam sale in mid-2017 and played through it for about a week and a half in Jan 2018. I actually don't remember why I decided to give this a shot, but presumably my experience with the Uncharted games had to do with it.

This game follows a young and inexperienced Lara Croft who gets stranded on a remote Japanese island in pursuit of the lost kingdom of Yamatai and she has to deal with a violent cult on the island. The gameplay is similar to the Uncharted games in terms of traversal and combat, but this is more of an "open world" exploration game where you can backtrack and go back to previous areas to find collectibles and such. There are also some quality of life features like "survival instinct" which highlights interactable items in the environment (similar to Arkham series' "Detective Mode") and better auto-aiming in combat.

There is also a survival and crafting element, as Lara must improvise weapons and craft materials to help her along. There is also a bit of RPG progression; Lara earns experience which can unlock skills to improve her abilities.

I overall enjoyed this game though honestly right now I'm not remembering a lot of the details. I do know I managed to finish it on normal difficulty.

Playtime: 19.6 hours. [HLTB](https://howlongtobeat.com/game/10469) says main+sides at 15.5 hours and completionist runs are at 21 hours. I'm not particularly good at action games so it wouldn't surprise me if I am below average here.

Completion: 31 out of 50 achievements. Looks like most of the missing achievements are multiplayer-related so it's not surprising I didn't attempt those!

### Shadow of the Tomb Raider (2018)

I got a copy of this game off the [Dec 2019 Humble Bundle](https://www.humblebundle.com/membership/december-2019) and only decided to play through it this year when looking for games I could knock off the old backlog. This is the third game in the trilogy, I did not care about the franchise enough to want to play *Rise of the Tomb Raider* lol.

The gameplay here is largely the same, except with an expanded skill tree and more custom weapon attacks and such. The story follows Lara to South America where she accidentally triggers a Mayan apocalypse. There are now towns and merchants and the trail leads Lara to a hidden city where mysteriously the natives all speak excellent English alongside their own tongue and the white British girl somehow manages to blend in and even disguise herself as one of the bad guys at some point. The bad guys in this case are a cult dedicated to the serpent deity Kukulkan and determined to use the apocalypse to rewrite the world as they see fit. All of this feels much more mystical than the previous games somehow!

This game somehow feels a lot larger than the previous game. No longer stranded on an island, Lara and her companion Jonah travel across different locations during the quest. Feels very Indiana Jones! Though somehow you can fast travel back to a location that you had to take a plane to leave? Whatever.

This game took me a while to get through. I started playing it in late June and finished the main story in mid September. There's just so much stuff to find, challenge tombs to do, skills to unlock, and so on. 

I started playing this on Linux desktop but ran into some crashy issues and was able to resume on Steam Deck, where I played most of the rest of the run. I did have to switch back to desktop for one particular challenge tomb because rapidly aiming and firing your bow and arrow while using the Steam Deck's analog stick + trackpad was much more challenging than just using mouse and keyboard.

I think I got tired of this game about halfway through and for that reason I ended up just powering through to the end and not bothering with completing all the collectibles. Also, I completed my one run on "Smart & Resourceful" difficulty, mostly on the advice of the [trophy guide](https://www.playstationtrophies.org/game/shadow-of-the-tomb-raider/guide/). I think this is the "Easy mode" difficulty and I kind of regret playing through the game this way. I'm fine with Lara giving puzzle hints (some of the puzzles can be quite obtuse anyway) and combat is a bit easier (there's aim assist!), but my main complaint is that for exploration, interactable paths are marked with super obvious white paint and it's seriously immersion-breaking. I came into an ancient tomb untouched for centuries but there's white paint telling me where to go?!? I think the game lets you customize the difficulty level so I could have kept the easier combat and puzzles but have normal difficulty for exploration.

Playtime: 34.1 hours. That's already almost 1.75 times my playtime for the first game! HLTB says main+sides is at 23h while completionist runs are at 39h, so it's really a much larger game than the first one.

Completion: I did NOT get all the collectibles for this one. After finishing the main story I'm at 95% completion, so I guess I have the option of spending an additional 5hrs to do that, but I probably won't. There are usually map indicators telling you of missed things, but some of them can be tedious to figure out how to access or get to; I'm already tired of this game and want to move on.

In terms of achievements, I'm at 67 out of 99 achievements, which isn't too bad. The remaining missing ones are mostly related to completion, difficulty runs, combat stuff, doing challenge tomb time attacks, etc.
