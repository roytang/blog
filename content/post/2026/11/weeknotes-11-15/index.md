### The World

The world had quite a week!

NVidia CEO Jensen Huang was forced to resign after details of [an affair with a suspected Chinese spy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal) (connected to a company that would import NVidia tech to China) came to light. Later two more alleged affairs with employees came out! NVidia stocks have tanked, and with Huang out, we are starting to get more details about [how they engineered the current RAM scarcity crisis](https://www.techpowerup.com/354814/nvidia-allegedly-engineered-scarcity-by-funding-ai-startups-to-buy-its-gpus). I know it would wreak chaos on the economy, but I would still like all of these AI-focused companies and their circular financing to crash compleely.

On Friday the 13th, the corporate world was rocked by a widespread outage of Microsoft services such as Outlook, Exchange, Office 365 etc. As someone who no longer walks in the corporate sphere I was not personally affected, but I watched the complaints and memes unfold across multiple friend groups. (Honestly, IDK why people would still be using MS Office in 2026 when better alternatives exist.) I think the outage lasted almost two days? Supposedly costing companies millions in lost productivity and such and such. In an ideal world, a big outage like this would teach people not to rely on big centralized corporate subscription based platforms. Sigh. I'm not sure if MS has already released an official explanation of the cause, but like many people I suspect vibe coding.

Then right after that, the US experienced a nationwide "ground stop" of flights ordered by the FAA. Thousands of flights were cancelled and many were stranded at airports. At first they were vague about the reason, but many suspected it would be related to [a recent upgrade to an AI air traffic tool](https://gizmodo.com/the-faa-is-rolling-out-its-ai-air-traffic-tool-nationwide-2000839347) just a few days prior. Later authorities would claim it would because some old tech was being upgraded and had communications problems, but [people suspect there is more to it](https://www.commondreams.org/news/duffy-ground-stop-explanation).

Also this one is a bit more niche and gaming-focused, but gaming storefront Humble Bundle decided this week to deploy an obviously vibe-coded website redesign that just seemed to break so many things and for a while the relevant subreddit was mostly posts skewering the new redesign. I used to buy a bunch of their bundles back in the 2010s and I still had some unredeemed keys and I decided to take the opportunity to navigate the now-terrible library pages to redeem a couple of dozen Steam keys. This kind of cost-cutting nonsense makes me think the company is not long for this world! Luckily I've already backed up most of the content I've purchased from them, where possible.
