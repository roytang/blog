**Marvel Tokon: Fighting Souls** is a fun little 4v4 fighter in the vein of the old Marvel vs Capcom series, but a bit different. It's developed by Arc System Works, which has a pretty good track record when it comes to this style of fighting games. I played the open beta of this a few weeks before release and it was fun (though I think the beta only let you use a few characters), but I was not inclined to actually pay money for it because:

- I am disinclined to give any money to Sony given [some of their recent decisions](/2026/07/physical-games/).
- I did not want to play multiplayer with this game on PS5 because for some reason network performance is always significantly worse for me on PSN than on PC (also, playing multiplayer requires giving money to Sony, something I have found mildly offensive since the PS4 era).
- I did not want to play multiplayer with this game on Steam because reportedly the PC port is pretty bad AND it doesn't work on Linux.
- Actually, I probably don't want to play too much multiplayer with this game regardless because these kinds of ArcSys games tend to be very fast-paced as compared to games like Street Fighter, and I tend to be pretty bad at them. 

So after the beta, I was NOT expecting to play the game again. Luckily, my friend lent me his PS5 again since last month, and he had bought a disc copy (hooray physical media!) of *Tokon* (apparently this word literally means "fighting souls" so it's redunant?) and left it with me, so I decided to give it a shot. (Also I didn't really feel like any more [Final Fantasy VII Rebirth](/2026/10/ff7rebirth/) so if I didn't play Tokon, the console might just have idled in our living room.)

### Excellent Graphics

The very first thing one would notice about this game: it's absolutely gorgeous. ArcSys has a great record with this kind of "anime fighter" for good reason. The character designs and animations are great and fun to watch, even when I'm getting beaten up a lot.

### Character Selection

My main gripe about this game is that considering it's a 4v4 fighter, the character selection roster is pretty small. The base game launched with a mere twenty characters. That's only 5 teams! That means if you were playing this online, there's not a lot of variation in teams and you will likely be facing the same characters again often. And they are planning 4 DLC characters per season, so the roster is not going to grow too quickly anytime soon.

The 5 initial teams (story-wise) are: Avengers, X-Men, "Amazing Guardians" (Spider-Man-focused), Samurai Outriders (Ghost Rider-focused), and The Knights of Doom ("villains"). Given that it's a 4v4 fighter, there is one obvious 6th team they could have added: The Fantastic Four! Why not?!?

Despite the low roster count, I did enjoy some of their character selection. There's the usual popular characters (Spider-Man, Wolverine, Deadpool, etc) but also a good number of off-beat deeper cuts. We get Shuri as Black Panther instead of T'Challa, the X-Men get Magik and Danger (?!?), Peni Parker is on the Amazing team, our Ghost Rider is Robbie Reyes, and we get Carnage instead of Venom. (My nephews still keep calling him Venom!)

They've also announced the roster for the first year of DLC characters, and there's some spicy choices there as well: Phoenix Cyclops (!!), Thor (Jane Foster) (!), Venom (the most boring addition), and Jubilee (not sure how to feel about this). Unlike the initial 5 "story teams", these DLC characters don't seem to form a coherent team though, IDK what's up with that or whether they get their own single-player content or what.

### Easy to Pick Up

The game is also super beginner-friendly and accessible with auto combos and shortcuts that make it so even people who've never picked up a fighting game before can have some fun.

As an example, I have two nephews aged 8 and 6 years old who have very little video gaming exposure (they don't get to play video games at home), so when they come over to visit I often let them play some games. I was able to hand them each a controller and drop them into a Versus match and they were able to get right into the fighting without any sort of tutorial or anything like that. They easily figured out which buttons to press and could pull off fancy-looking supers with no problem!

The last time the PS5 was here I let them play some Lego Marvel and that was a bit annoying because I would constantly need to help them progress through the stages. With Tokon, we just left them alone to play a few matches!

### Single-Player Content

Given that I did not expect to play a lot of multiplayer, I'm happy to report that this game has some good single-player content. There's a fairly easy-to-follow tutorial, and there's an episode mode where you play each of the "story teams" through the game's story. The story is written by comics scribe Kieron Gillen and is appropriately a very "comic book" story. The story cut scenes are told mostly through comic panels, and each team's story has a different artist team, which can make for some wild variations in art style.

The story's final boss is The Champion - an obscure comics character I did not even remember existed, probably the last Elder of the Universe I would be able to name. He is however totally appropriate as the final boss of a fighting game, and he can give you some trouble at the end of each story line. Beating all five story lines unlocks a tougher version of the final boss! (And also makes The Champion selectable in versus games.) I have yet to beat that tougher version; most probably a skill issue, but also I read that the AI reads your inputs!!

My favorite among the five story lines is the Knights of Doom one because of course Doom has to do things his way!!

There's also a bunch of other standard FG content - trials, survival mode, training, etc. As is typical for me, I had trouble executing the more difficult trials for each character.

There's also a weird little mode called Arcadia which involves alternating between standard battles and walking an avatar around a 3d "theme park" of sorts and attacking other players. I did not like it, matches took too long.

### A Love Letter to Fighting Games

I did enjoy this game despite being very bad at it, and I'm glad ArcSys handled it because they know fighting games and they put a lot of love into the product. ### Playtime Completion

I was able to do achievement completion for this game!

{{< photos 20261115 >}}

I almost never do achievement completion for fighting games because there's usually a multiplayer component AND a skill barrier for me, but Tokon was approachable enough and did not require too much multiplayer stuff. The only achievement that gave me trouble was one where I needed to do 5 crossover reflects. I didn't have anyone to boost this with, so I had to rely on somehow getting the computer to do the crossovers, and even when they did, my piss-poor reaction time meant I very rarely got the reflect in on time. This took me many matches, but at least it helped grind out the 500 battles achievement.

Total playtime I spent on this game was around 25 hours.

### The Future

I already said I would't buy this game, but I enjoyed it enough that I would consider buying it in the future if:

- there is a big discount
- I can get all the DLC characters as well
- they fix the Steam version performance AND add Linux support

We'll see if that ever happens, but it feels unlikely and even then I suspect it would be AT LEAST five years out.
